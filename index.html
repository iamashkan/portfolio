02 — About

About

Ashkan Aghamoali is an XR developer and digital twin engineer with the Mechanical Intelligence Research Group at London South Bank University (LSBU), where the AR work grew out of research combining machine learning with bio-inspired structural engineering.

He develops Python-based automation around CAE / Abaqus simulations, trains machine-learning models to predict structural behaviour from large simulation datasets, and applies optimization and sensitivity analysis to inform design decisions. In parallel, he builds Extended Reality (AR/VR) applications for visualising simulations, finite-element analysis and parametric models.

He holds an MSc in Digital Electronic Systems and a BSc in Electrical & Electronics Engineering from the University of Guilan, and is a gold-medal-winning member of the International Federation of Inventors' Associations (IFIA).

Real-time digital twin generation on iOS

Turning an iPhone into an industrial scanning tool: room geometry captured live with ARKit + RoomPlan, equipment detected and labelled on-device with Core ML / Vision, nameplates read by OCR, and each scanned asset bound to a real device through an edge gateway over MQTT. 01
Simulation · hardware-ready

Sizing a robot cell against a shredder

Circular recovery of end-of-life electric motors · UR5e · ROS 2 Jazzy · MoveIt 2 · Gazebo

End-of-life motors today are mostly shredded, with their copper recovered by eddy-current separation. So the useful question was never whether a cobot can take a motor apart — it is the motor size above which doing so beats shredding. That framing decided the architecture: the cell is modelled per separable component rather than per motor, so the crossover can be read straight off the results. A UR5e with a Robotiq 2F-85 gripper sorts eleven components across reuse / repair / replace / recycle stations, MoveIt 2 plans the 17-step pick-and-place workflow through ros2_control, and a Python decision engine scores each part's health and risk from a Digital Product Passport and sensor stream.

Limit Perception is mocked. The motion planning, workcell and decision engine are real; what a camera actually sees on a corroded motor is not something I could simulate honestly, so I did not pretend to.

02
Simulation · real datasets

What an RGB camera cannot tell you about a battery

End-of-life lithium-ion recovery and robotic sorting · Unity · FastAPI · scikit-learn

The EU Battery Regulation makes a digital battery passport mandatory for EV, industrial and light-transport batteries from 18 February 2027, which fixes the data model the whole system has to emit — so the passport was built first and the sorting line second, not the other way round. A Unity control twin streams each cell's image to a FastAPI backend that classifies form factor, draws a state-of-health profile from NASA PCoE aging data and scores risk; a decision engine then routes every battery to reuse / remanufacture / recycle / quarantine under selectable policy modes, with an ABB CRB-style cobot sorting it on the line. Built on a real RecyBat24 image subset rather than synthetic data.

Limit Cell chemistry — LFP against NMC — cannot be read off an RGB image; a real plant needs XRF or spectroscopy. The classifier is therefore scoped to form factor, and chemistry is carried by the passport instead of being inferred.

03
Simulation · edge-to-cloud

Designing against a baseline that wins on energy

Emergency-aware adaptive lighting for a 6-lane smart motorway · STM32 · MQTT · Unity 6

The emergency case is the thesis. When an ambulance, police or fire vehicle is detected, the system raises a 100% safety corridor ahead of it — in that direction only. Outside emergencies the road holds a configurable eco level and brightens just the luminaires ahead of each vehicle, while modelling energy, CO₂, luminaire health, remaining useful life and Re-X lifecycle decisions. It is built across the full stack rather than as a controller in isolation: telemetry over MQTT into a Streamlit operations dashboard, a five-node STM32 edge-controller network on RS485 / CAN simulated in Wokwi, and a Unity 6 night-highway twin.

Limit Many highway authorities now switch motorway lighting off entirely between midnight and 05:30. Against that baseline this system loses on energy outright — which is why it is argued on emergency response and luminaire lifecycle, and not on kilowatt-hours saved.
